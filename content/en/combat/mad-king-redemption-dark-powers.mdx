## The advertised identity: brawler first, roguelite second

The Steam description leads with combat: slaying hordes of enemies using brawler-style combat built on old-school controls, with a retro presentation layered over 3D mechanics. The roguelite elements are described as "deep," and the progression loop is framed as upgrading heroes and unlocking multiple skills.

Read that list carefully. It does not say how many heroes exist. It does not describe a skill tree's shape, how many skills are unlockable, how runs are structured, or what carries over between attempts. Those are the details players most want in a roguelite, and they are the details the store page leaves open. That is not a red flag by itself — it is normal for a page that was published for an Early Access launch — but it means you should treat the genre label as a direction rather than a specification.

What you *can* lean on is the combination claim. Mixing brawler combat with roguelite progression usually means the moment-to-moment fighting is authored and deliberate (fixed enemy types, readable hitboxes, combo timing) while the run structure and upgrades introduce variance. How heavily Mad King Redemption leans into either half is something only play will tell you.

Mad King Redemption launched in Early Access, which means the developer has explicitly reserved the right to change systems.
