## What "roguelite elements" reasonably implies here

The store page uses the phrase directly, so it is fair to reason about what that typically means without asserting specifics. In a side-scrolling brawler with roguelite structure, you should expect run-based progression, some form of permanent upgrade that persists across attempts, and a reason to repeat earlier content.

What you should not do is assume a particular meta-progression currency, a fixed number of runs to unlock a skill, or a specific death penalty. None of that is documented. The honest approach for a new player is to play the first several runs without a plan, note which upgrades appear in the in-run pool, and note separately which upgrades persist between runs. Those two pools are the real structure of any roguelite, and observing them yourself takes less time than reading a guide built on someone else's patch.

If you want to verify a claim about skills or heroes, the reliable checks are the in-game codex, the upgrade screens themselves, and the official Steam page. Community wikis and video guides are secondary and should be labeled as such in your own head.

## Practical approach for a new player in Early Access

Early Access changes the calculus. You are not optimizing a solved game; you are learning a moving target. A few habits help regardless of what the next patch does.

- Read the store page description once, in full. It is short, and it is the only place the developer states intent directly.
- Treat upgrade choices as experiments, not commitments. Note what a skill does before you invest in a second tier of it.
- Watch for patch notes on the Steam news feed. In Early Access, the patch notes are more valuable than any static guide.
- Do not chase undocumented numbers. If a damage value or drop rate is not in the game's own interface or the store page, it is not something you can rely on.

The brawler layer is the part least likely to change dramatically, since old-school controls and retro feel are core to the pitch. The roguelite layer is the part most likely to shift. Weight your learning accordingly.
