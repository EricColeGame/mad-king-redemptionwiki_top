The name "Mad King Redemption" invites a specific expectation: a fallen ruler, a quest to undo what he did, and a story about whether a madman can be saved. The actual game, developed by SECRET MISSION and released in Early Access on 14 May 2026, is something a little different. According to its official Steam store page, you are not the king. ## What the Official Store Page Says the Game Is

The Steam listing for Mad King Redemption (app 2369580) is the authority on the game's identity. It describes a kingdom fallen to the Mad King, who used forbidden magic in an attempt to resuscitate the queen and lost his sanity in the process. That single sentence does most of the narrative work: the catastrophe is personal, magical, and self-inflicted. The king is not a foreign invader. He is the reason the kingdom is broken.

Mechanically, the page calls it a side-scrolling beat'em'up with deep roguelite elements. It advertises brawler-style combat against hordes of enemies, hero upgrades, multiple unlockable skills, and a deliberate blend of old-school controls and retro feel with modern 3D mechanics. Those are the developer's own claims, and they are the only reliable baseline for what you will find on day one of Early Access.

What the page does not publish is equally important. There is no complete hero roster, no skill tree, no boss list, and no damage values. If you see a guide elsewhere claiming to have the full breakdown, treat it as a third-party observation rather than developer documentation.

## Why "Redemption" Is the Central Design Question

The keyword matters because it frames how you read every system in the game. A roguelite is already a genre built on repetition and recovery: you fail, you return, you get a little stronger. Layering that onto a fallen kingdom ruled by a king who broke reality for love gives the loop a thematic weight that a generic dungeon crawler lacks.

The store description does not confirm that the Mad King is a playable character, nor does it describe an ending in which he is redeemed. It describes him as the cause of the collapse and the enemies you fight as hordes. So the honest reading is this: redemption in Mad King Redemption is available to the player's run, not necessarily to the monarch. Whether the narrative eventually turns on that irony is something the store page simply does not document. The in-game codex and any future developer updates are where that would surface.

## Brawler Feel With Roguelite Structure

The most useful thing to understand before buying is how those two halves interact. A classic side-scrolling beat'em'up is about positioning, crowd control, and reading enemy patterns at close range. A roguelite is about run-based progression, randomized rewards, and persistent upgrades that carry between attempts.

Combining them means your moment-to-moment skill matters, but so does the meta layer. The official page confirms hero upgrades and skill unlocks, which implies persistent progression outside a single run. It does not confirm how many runs a typical unlock takes, whether upgrades are currency-based, or whether skills are shared across heroes or unique to each. Those are exactly the details an Early Access build can change, so any number you read today should be re-checked in-game after a patch.

The "old-school controls and retro feel with 3D mechanics" phrase is worth taking at face value too. It suggests hand-drawn or pixel-adjacent 2D action presented in a 3D-rendered space, or 3D models on a 2D plane. The store page does not specify which, and screenshots are the fastest way to judge whether the art direction works for you.
