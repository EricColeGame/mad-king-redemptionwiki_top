Mad King Redemption is a side-scrolling beat'em'up with roguelite elements from developer SECRET MISSION. It entered Early Access on 14 May 2026, according to its [official Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2369580/). The premise is stated plainly there: the kingdom has fallen to the Mad King, who turned to forbidden magic in an attempt to resuscitate the queen, lost his sanity in the process, and now rules over a realm the player has to fight through.

That setup gives the title a resonance its name already hints at. Redemption is the loaded word. Is the Mad King a villain to be destroyed, a tragedy to be understood, or something the game leaves for the player to decide? The store page does not answer that, and this article will not pretend otherwise. What it can do is separate what SECRET MISSION advertises from what players report, and be honest about the wide middle ground that Early Access always leaves open.

## What the Store Page Advertises

Official materials describe a beat'em'up that combines old-school controls and a retro feel with 3D mechanics. Combat is brawler-style: the player slays hordes of enemies across a fallen kingdom. Layered on top are what the developer calls deep roguelite elements, along with hero upgrades and multiple unlockable skills.

Those are the advertised pillars, and they cluster around a familiar loop. You pick a hero, fight through enemy waves, die or finish a run, then spend what you earned on permanent progression before going again. The roguelite framing matters because it changes how you read the title. Redemption in a run-based game is rarely a single story beat; it is usually something you accumulate across attempts.

It is worth being precise about the limits of that description. The store page does not publish a complete hero roster, a skill tree, a boss list, or any damage values. If you have seen a "full character tier list" or "exact stat breakdown" elsewhere, treat it as unofficial. The game's own in-game codex is the authority on numbers, and it can change between patches.

## The Redemption Angle

The keyword most people search around this game is redemption, and it is fair to ask what the game does with it. The verified description supports a clear reading: the Mad King's fall is motivated by grief. He used forbidden magic to try to bring the queen back. That attempt cost him his sanity and, implicitly, his kingdom.

Everything beyond that origin is undocumented. The store page does not state whether the Mad King is the final boss, whether he is playable, whether the campaign offers a path to save him, or whether "redemption" refers to him at all rather than to the heroes restoring the realm. A player who wants a definitive answer should watch for in-game narrative text and the codex rather than trusting secondhand summaries.

Third-party guides have speculated about multiple endings and branching routes tied to the king's fate. Those are community observations, not developer documentation, and they should be weighed accordingly. In Early Access, story content is often the last thing to be finalized.

## Early Access Realities

Early Access is a promise with a caveat. Mad King Redemption released in that state on 14 May 2026, which means systems are subject to change. Balance passes can alter how a skill feels, progression pacing can shift, and content that does not exist today may appear later.

That has two consequences for anyone researching the game. First, any guide with precise figures should carry a date, and you should check whether it predates the current build. Second, absence of information is not the same as absence of content. A hero roster that is not published on the store page may still be discoverable in-game.

To verify anything yourself, the most reliable path is straightforward: read the [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2369580/) for the developer's own description, then open the game and consult the in-game codex for numbers. For roadmap or patch intent, the developer's own announcements outrank aggregator sites and fan wikis. And be skeptical of any page that lists exact drop rates, secret codes, or a complete boss order for an Early Access title whose store page documents none of it.

## Is It Worth Following?

If you like brawlers and you like roguelite progression, the combination here is coherent rather than gimmicky: retro controls and 3D mechanics in service of a horde-clearing loop, with upgrades that give repeated runs a point. If you are here specifically for a redemption story, the honest position is that the premise supports one and the marketing leans on it, but the store page does not document how, or whether, that arc resolves. That is a question for the game itself, and for however SECRET MISSION chooses to develop it during Early Access.
