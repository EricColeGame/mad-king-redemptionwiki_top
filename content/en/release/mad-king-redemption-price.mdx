"Mad King Redemption" is a title that promises a lot in four words: a villain, a downfall, and a shot at something better. SECRET MISSION's side-scrolling beat'em'up, available in Early Access on [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2369580/) since 14 May 2026, builds its premise around exactly that tension. A kingdom has fallen to the Mad King, who turned to forbidden magic in an attempt to revive the queen and lost his sanity in the process. Players fight through the wreckage of that decision, clearing hordes of enemies with brawler-style combat while upgrading heroes and unlocking skills along the way.

## How the Beat'em'up and Roguelite Layers Fit Together

Side-scrolling beat'em'ups traditionally ask you to memorize enemy patterns across fixed stages. Roguelite structure usually asks the opposite: randomized runs, incremental permanent upgrades, and repetition with variation. The official description places Mad King Redemption in both camps, with deep roguelite elements layered onto brawler combat.

The practical implication is straightforward. You should expect each run to feed something back into your account — upgraded heroes, unlocked skills — while the moment-to-moment fighting stays rooted in classic controls and a retro presentation rendered with 3D mechanics. What the store page does not specify is how permanent progression is gated, whether runs are fully randomized or partially authored, or how many skills exist per hero. Those are Early Access details that can shift between builds.

## Reading Third-Party Guides Carefully

Community guides for Early Access games tend to appear fast and age badly. A third-party guide might describe an upgrade path that a mid-Early Access patch has already reworked, or present one player's preferred build as if it were a documented system. That does not make those guides useless — they can show you what a run looks like in practice — but the attribution matters. Treat build advice as one player's tested opinion, not as developer-confirmed data.

The safest verification loop is simple: read the claim, then check it in-game against the codex, then check the official Steam page or madkingredemption.com for any developer statement. If a number appears in a guide but nowhere in the game or on an official channel, treat it as unverified.

## What Early Access Means for What You Read Here

Mad King Redemption launched into Early Access, which by definition means the developers intend to keep changing the game. Systems described today may be rebalanced, expanded, or replaced. The verified facts in this article — developer name, release date, premise, and advertised feature categories — are the stable part. Anything more granular is a moving target.

That is not a reason to avoid the game; it is a reason to set expectations correctly. If you are the kind of player who wants a finished, fully documented system before committing, Early Access is a poor fit for you regardless of how strong the core combat loop looks.
