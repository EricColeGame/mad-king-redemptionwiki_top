## Why the Premise Matters More Than It Looks

The setup is doing real work. The Mad King is not a villain who wanted power for its own sake. According to the official description, he used forbidden magic to try to resuscitate the queen and lost his sanity as a result. That gives the enemy hordes a thematic reason to exist: you are cutting through the consequences of grief that went too far.

For a beat'em'up, that framing is unusually load-bearing. The genre traditionally runs on thin justification, and that is fine. Here the story gives the roguelite loop a kind of narrative logic. Each run is another push into a kingdom that ruined itself, and each upgrade is a small accumulation of capability against a ruler who traded his mind for a spell that did not work the way he hoped.

Whether the game delivers on that in cutscenes, dialogue, or environmental storytelling is not something the store page documents. You will have to play the opening hours or watch unedited gameplay to judge the execution. What is documented is the intent.
