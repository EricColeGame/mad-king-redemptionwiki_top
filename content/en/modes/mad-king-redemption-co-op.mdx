## Roguelite Structure Versus Beat'em'Up Structure

These two genres pull in different directions, and understanding the tension is the fastest way to understand this game.

A traditional beat'em'up is about execution inside a fixed stage: you learn enemy patterns, spacing, and crowd control, and you replay the same corridor until you stop getting hit. A roguelite is about variance between runs: you accept that some attempts are stronger than others, and you build toward a combination that carries you further than last time.

When a game claims to combine them, the roguelite layer usually does one of two jobs:

| Approach | What it changes | What it preserves |
|---|---|---|
| Run-based upgrades | Your power curve resets between attempts | Enemy patterns and level layout |
| Meta-progression | Permanent unlocks carry across runs | The need to execute combat well |

The advertised phrase "upgrading heroes and unlocking multiple skills" points toward the second category — persistent progression sitting on top of repeatable combat. That is a reasonable reading, but the store page does not state whether hero upgrades are permanent, per-run, or a mix. Community discussion on the Steam forums and third-party guides may describe how it currently works, but those are observations about a specific build, not developer documentation, and they can be invalidated by any patch.

## Reading Early Access Honestly

The phrase "Early Access" is doing real work here. It means systems can change, content can be rebalanced, and features described on the store page may be expanded, reworked, or delayed.

For a roguelite specifically, Early Access has an outsized effect on one thing: meta-progression. When a developer tunes permanent upgrades or shifts the power curve, every guide written before that patch becomes partially wrong. Combat fundamentals — spacing, enemy tells, crowd management — tend to survive balance changes better than any specific build does.

So the practical hierarchy is:

- Most stable: learning enemy behavior and crowd control
- Moderately stable: which broad skill categories feel strong
- Least stable: exact stat breakpoints, tier lists, and unlock thresholds

Invest your learning time in that order and a patch is less likely to invalidate your effort.

## A Reasonable Approach for New Players

If the premise appeals to you — a fallen kingdom, a king undone by grief and forbidden magic, brawler combat with roguelite progression — the sensible entry point is straightforward. Play a few runs without chasing an optimized build. Pay attention to which of your available skills you actually use under pressure, not which one looks strongest on paper. Use the in-game codex to check what something does before you assume a community post has it right.

Fighting hordes in a beat'em'up is largely a problem of positioning: you are managing how many enemies can reach you at once. That skill transfers across characters and builds, which makes it the best early investment regardless of how the roguelite layer currently stacks up.
