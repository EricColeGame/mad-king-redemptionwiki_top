Mad King Redemption is a side-scrolling beat'em'up with roguelite elements from developer SECRET MISSION, released in Early Access on 14 May 2026. Its premise is a fallen kingdom: the Mad King turned to forbidden magic in an attempt to resuscitate the queen, lost his sanity in the process, and left the realm overrun. You play as heroes fighting through those hordes.

That is the short version, and it is almost entirely drawn from the official [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2369580/). ## What the store page actually advertises

The official Steam description is specific about a few things and silent about many others. Here is what it commits to:

- **Genre framing:** a beat'em'up that combines old-school controls and a retro feel with 3D mechanics.
- **Combat:** brawler-style fighting against hordes of enemies.
- **Roguelite structure:** the store describes "deep roguelite elements."
- **Progression:** upgrading heroes and unlocking multiple skills.
- **Narrative setup:** the kingdom has fallen to the Mad King, who used forbidden magic to try to resuscitate the queen and lost his sanity.

Notice what is absent. The store page does not publish a complete hero list, a skill tree, a boss list, or any damage values. It does not describe meta-progression currencies, run length, difficulty tiers, or unlock levels. That is not an oversight you can patch over by reading a wiki — it means those systems are either still in flux or documented only inside the game.

## Separating advertised features from community guesswork

This is the part where a lot of coverage goes wrong. The phrase "deep roguelite elements" is a marketing summary, not a spec sheet. It tells you there is run-based structure and some form of persistent progression, because that is what roguelite generally means. It does not tell you whether upgrades persist between runs, whether they are hero-specific, or how unlocking works.

Third-party guides have started appearing since launch, and they can be genuinely useful for orientation — where a menu lives, roughly how a run flows, which early upgrade feels strong. Treat them as secondary observations from other players, not as developer documentation. If a guide states a specific multiplier, an exact skill node cost, or a full hero roster, ask where that number came from. If the answer is "datamining" or "I tested it," it is community data that can change with any Early Access patch.

The reliable hierarchy looks like this:

| Source | What it's good for | What it can't tell you |
|---|---|---|
| Official Steam page | Genre, premise, advertised features, release state | Hero list, skill tree, damage values, unlock levels |
| In-game codex | Current mechanics as the build implements them | Future changes |
| Third-party guides | Orientation, general approaches, community impressions | Anything presented as developer-documented fact |

## Why "redemption" matters for how you read the game

The title is doing thematic work that affects expectations. The kingdom fell *because* the Mad King tried to bring the queen back with forbidden magic — the catastrophe and the motive are the same event. That framing suggests a story about consequence rather than a simple "kill the evil tyrant" setup, though the store page does not detail how far the narrative goes or whether the King is the final encounter.

Practical takeaway: do not assume the boss structure. The store page does not list bosses, and it does not confirm the Mad King appears as a fightable enemy at any particular point. If you are planning a run around an expected final boss, you are planning around something undocumented. Check the in-game codex or simply play through and observe.

## Practical advice for Early Access players

Because the systems are not fully documented publicly, your best approach is empirical rather than prescriptive:

1. **Read the in-game codex first.** It reflects the build you are actually playing, which no external page can guarantee.
2. **Test upgrades rather than trusting tier lists.** Community rankings written before a patch may not describe the current balance.
3. **Expect change.** Early Access means hero tuning, skill costs, and run structure can shift. Anything you read today, including this article, describes a snapshot.
4. **Check the official page after patches.** The [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2369580/) is the authority on what the developer claims, and it updates alongside the build.

None of this guarantees success in a given run — roguelite outcomes depend on your execution, the build you assemble, and randomness the store page does not describe.
