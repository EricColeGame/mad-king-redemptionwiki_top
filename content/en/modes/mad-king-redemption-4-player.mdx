Mad King Redemption is a side-scrolling beat'em'up with roguelite elements from developer SECRET MISSION, released in Early Access on 14 May 2026 and sold on [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2369580/). The premise is compact: the kingdom has fallen to the Mad King, who turned to forbidden magic in an attempt to resuscitate the queen and lost his sanity in the process. You fight through hordes of enemies, upgrade heroes, and unlock skills across runs.

Because the game is in Early Access, the most useful thing this article can do is separate what the store page actually advertises from what players report and what simply is not documented yet. ## The advertised premise and combat style

The Steam listing describes a fallen kingdom, a Mad King undone by forbidden magic, and brawler-style combat against hordes of enemies. Two phrases do a lot of work there: "old-school controls and retro feel" combined with "3D mechanics." In practice, that signals a 2D-plane beat'em'up — you move, you punch, you crowd-control — rendered with 3D assets rather than pixel art.

The store page also advertises "deep roguelite elements," upgrading heroes and unlocking multiple skills. That is the developer's own framing. It does not specify how many heroes exist, how the upgrade economy works, or what a skill unlock costs.

## How roguelite structure likely shapes a run

The phrase "roguelite elements" in a brawler usually means three things: runs are self-contained, death sends you back, and some progress persists between attempts. The store page supports the persistence half explicitly with "upgrading heroes and unlocking multiple skills" — that is meta-progression language, not per-run language.

What is not documented is how generous that persistence is. A roguelite can lean toward short runs with heavy permanent upgrades, or long runs where most of your power is found inside the run itself. Mad King Redemption does not say which. The honest answer is that you find out by playing, and that the balance may shift while the game is in Early Access.

Practically, that means your first sessions are better spent learning enemy patterns and crowd management than chasing an optimal unlock order. In beat'em'ups, positioning against a horde is usually a bigger lever than raw stats, and nothing in the store description contradicts that.

## Reading community advice without getting burned

Third-party guides for Early Access games appear fast and age badly. Two habits keep you from wasting time on them.

First, check whether the guide states a version or date. Early Access balance changes invalidate specifics quickly. A guide written in May 2026 may describe a game that no longer exists by fall.

Second, check whether the claim is something the game could even document. "The Mad King used forbidden magic" is premise. "Hero X has the highest DPS" is a measurement, and measurements drift with patches. Community build advice is not developer-documented data, and treating it as such is how players end up following a stale tier list.

If you want to verify a claim, load the game, open the in-game codex, and read the current entry. If the codex does not list it, the claim is someone's inference. The [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2369580/) is the other check — it is where SECRET MISSION posts its own description and any Early Access notes.

## Who this game is actually for

The combination narrows the audience in a useful way. If you want a brawler with a retro arcade rhythm and you are comfortable with runs resetting, the advertised design points at you. If you want a hand-authored campaign with a fixed hero roster and no repetition, "roguelite elements" is a warning, not a feature.

The Early Access label adds a second filter. Systems described today can change. Upgrading heroes and unlocking skills are advertised features, but their tuning is exactly the kind of thing that moves during development. Buying in now means buying into a version, not a finished design.

None of this is a promise about quality either way. It is a description of what has been advertised and what has not.
