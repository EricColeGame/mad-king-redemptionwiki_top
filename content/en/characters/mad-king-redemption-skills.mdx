## What the Store Page Says About the Mad King

The verified official description positions the Mad King as the cause of the kingdom's collapse rather than a distant final boss. His forbidden magic failed in the worst way: the queen was not restored, and his mind broke. The result is a realm overrun by enemies that you fight through as a side-scrolling beat'em'up.

That framing is useful for setting expectations. This is a fallen-kingdom setup, not a heroic rescue story. The gameplay is about cutting through the consequences of one ruler's grief-driven decision.

It is worth being precise about limits here. The Steam page does not publish a boss list, does not name the queen, and does not describe a story campaign structure. ## Why "Redemption" Changes How You Should Approach Runs

The word redemption implies recovery, and that has practical weight in a roguelite. If the kingdom fell because of one man's forbidden magic, the fights you take on are framed as restoration work rather than conquest.

In practice, though, the store page does not define a redemption meter, a morality system, or a story-branching mechanic tied to that word. Nothing in the official description says your choices affect the Mad King's fate. Players who assume a redemption mechanic exists may be reading the title more literally than the source supports.

The safer interpretation: "redemption" is thematic framing for a beat'em'up roguelite about fighting through a ruined kingdom. If redemption turns out to be a real system, the in-game codex will say so, not a third-party summary.
