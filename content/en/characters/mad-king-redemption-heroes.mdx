Mad King Redemption is a side-scrolling beat'em'up with roguelite elements from developer SECRET MISSION. It entered Early Access on Steam on 14 May 2026, and its store page frames the premise simply: a kingdom has fallen to the Mad King, who turned to forbidden magic in an attempt to resuscitate the queen, lost his sanity in the process, and now rules over a realm that the player must fight through. That setup matters more than it might first appear, because it shapes what the game is actually about: not a single villain to topple in one clean run, but a broken kingdom you push back through repeatedly.

This article sticks to what the developer advertises on the [official Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2369580/), separates that from community observation, and is upfront about what the store page does not document. The game is in Early Access, so systems can and likely will change.

## What the Store Page Actually Advertises

According to the official Steam listing, Mad King Redemption offers:

That is the whole of the advertised feature set. Notably, the store page does not publish a complete hero roster, a skill tree, a boss list, damage values, drop rates, or unlock levels. The "Mad King" in the title refers to the antagonist whose forbidden magic broke the kingdom. The "Redemption" half is interpretive rather than documented: the store description does not spell out whether the player redeems the king, the kingdom, or a hero. Early Access store text is often deliberately vague, and this one is.

## Why the Roguelite Layer Changes How You Play

A traditional beat'em'up asks you to memorize a stage and clear it. A roguelite asks you to accept that a run may end badly and still be worth something. Mad King Redemption sits in both camps, and that combination has practical consequences:

| Aspect | Typical beat'em'up | Mad King Redemption (as advertised) |
|---|---|---|
| Progression | Stage-by-stage, fixed | Runs feed hero upgrades and skill unlocks |
| Failure | Restart the stage | Likely resets the run, not your meta-progression |
| Content | Hand-tuned encounters | Horde combat with roguelite variation |
| Controls | Arcade-style | Old-school controls with 3D mechanics |

The exact behavior of failure — what you keep, what you lose — is not documented on the store page. Players should verify it directly in the game, since roguelite meta-progression rules are exactly the kind of thing that gets tuned during Early Access.

## The Combat Feel: Retro Controls, 3D Space

The store description emphasizes "old-school controls and retro feel with 3D mechanics." In practice, that combination usually means 2D-plane movement with depth, the classic beat'em'up approach where you walk up and down as well as left and right. That is an inference from the genre and the wording, not a documented control scheme. The store page does not list hotkeys, button mappings, or combo inputs.

If you are evaluating whether the combat suits you, the useful move is to check recent Steam reviews and the official page rather than rely on aggregated claims. The store page does not publish frame data or input lists.
