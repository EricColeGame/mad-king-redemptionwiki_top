Mad King Redemption is a side-scrolling beat'em'up with roguelite structure from developer SECRET MISSION, released in Early Access on 14 May 2026. That combination of genres is unusually crowded, and the store page for [Mad King Redemption on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2369580/) is refreshingly short on hype: it states a premise, a combat style, and a progression loop, and leaves most numbers unsaid.

That restraint matters when you are deciding whether to buy in. Because the game is in Early Access, anything not written down by the developer is genuinely unsettled, and a lot of the advice circulating about hero builds and skill unlocks is inference rather than documentation. ## The advertised premise, in the developer's own terms

According to the Steam store description, the kingdom has fallen to the Mad King. He used forbidden magic in an attempt to revive the queen, the attempt failed him, and he lost his sanity in the process. You are fighting through the wreckage of that decision.

This is worth stating plainly because the title invites a misreading. "Redemption" here is not obviously a story branch the store page promises, and SECRET MISSION does not describe a morality system, a forgiveness mechanic, or multiple endings. The word sits in the game's name, not in a documented feature list. If you are buying specifically because you expect a narrative redemption arc, treat that expectation as unverified until you see it in game.

What is documented is tone and framing: a ruined kingdom, a monarch broken by grief and forbidden magic, and horde combat against what that broke loose.

## Beat'em'up controls with roguelite runs

The store page describes brawler-style combat against hordes of enemies, old-school controls, a retro feel, and 3D mechanics layered behind the 2D-plane fighting. It also describes "deep roguelite elements," upgrading heroes, and unlocking multiple skills.

Read together, that suggests a familiar shape: short runs, persistent upgrades between them, and a roster you develop over time rather than a single fixed protagonist. The important word is "suggests." The store page does not publish a complete hero list, a skill tree, a boss list, damage values, or unlock levels, and this article will not invent them. Where a guide states "Hero X scales with Stat Y," that is a third-party observation about a specific build of an Early Access game, not developer documentation, and it can be invalidated by a patch.

## Why "redemption" is the hardest keyword to pin down

Most searches that combine this game's title with "redemption" are really asking one of three different questions, and they deserve different answers.

The first is narrative: does the Mad King get redeemed, and can the player influence that? The developer has not documented branching endings or a redemption mechanic. That does not mean none exists; it means the store page is not the authority on it.

The second is mechanical: is there a redemption or revival system, a way to restore lost progress or fallen allies, given that the king's own forbidden magic was an attempted resuscitation? Nothing in the advertised feature set describes such a system. Roguelite progression and "redemption" are not the same thing, and conflating them leads to disappointed purchases.

The third is thematic: does the game treat the king as a villain to defeat or a tragedy to understand? The store description frames him as a man undone by grief, which is characterization, not a mechanic.

The honest answer to all three is that the in-game codex and the current build are the authority, and both can change during Early Access.

## Practical Early Access reasoning

If you are weighing a purchase, work from the documented surface area and treat everything else as a bet.

- **You know you are getting** a side-scrolling beat'em'up with horde combat, retro-leaning controls, 3D mechanics, and roguelite progression with hero upgrades and unlockable skills.
- **You do not know** how many heroes exist, how deep the skill system runs, how many bosses there are, or how long a completed run takes, because SECRET MISSION has not published those figures.
- **You should assume** that balance and progression can shift between patches, since that is what Early Access means in practice.

The cheapest way to reduce uncertainty is to read the [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2369580/) in full, including recent patch notes and player reviews sorted by recent rather than overall. Reviews written against older builds describe a game that may no longer exist. Then check the in-game codex yourself once you are playing, because that is the only source that reflects the build actually installed on your machine.

## What to do with community build advice

Third-party guides are useful and should be labeled as such. When a guide tells you which hero to level first or which skill to prioritize, it is reporting one player's experience with one patch. That is legitimate information, and it is not the same category as a developer-published stat table.

A workable habit: note the date on any guide, cross-check it against the current patch, and confirm any specific number in the in-game codex before you commit resources to it. If a guide lists damage values or unlock levels that the store page and codex do not show, treat those numbers as unverified.
